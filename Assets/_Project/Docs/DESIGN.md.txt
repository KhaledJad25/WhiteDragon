==================================================
PART 1: THE GAME
==================================================

## Vision
First-person dark-fantasy roguelite. Inspired by the DESIGN PHILOSOPHY of The Binding of Isaac (simple stats, huge item synergy, room-by-room runs, meta-progression that widens the possibility space), in a different setting and camera. Tone: gritty and dark, in the spirit of Berserk, Darkest Dungeon, Dark Souls, and Elden Ring, toned down (Crimson Moon style) so assets are cheap to make and load fast.
We use our own names and mechanics. Isaac is a compass, not a template.

## Setting and story
- The player starts in, or has just escaped from, a tower.
- Goal of the full game: return, defeat the one who jailed the player, then face deeper dark entities unlocked by play.
- Two paths: Corruption (succumb to dark arts, Femto-style) and Fellowship (rally allies, Band of the Hawk style).
- Endings are determined by final boss defeated + alignment tier + story flags.

## Core loop
Enter a room, doors seal, clear the room, reward, move on. Floor boss, descend, repeat. Every run is seeded.

## Combat
- First character throws rocks. Unlimited ammo. Aimed from the camera center, with arc and range.
- No wind-up, recovery, stamina, or lockout. The only limit on throwing is a fire-rate interval.
- Items visibly and mechanically change the shot.
- Pacing: deliberate room-by-room tension and readable enemies (long telegraphs), but the player's own throwing stays snappy.

## Controls (keyboard and mouse only for now; gamepad later)
WASD move, mouse look, Space jump, Left Mouse throw (hold to repeat), E interact, Esc release or capture the cursor, F1 debug panel.

PART 2: PROGRESSION PHILOSOPHY (THE MOST IMPORTANT PART)
==================================================

## "Randomized runs, structured meta-progression"
Every run feels unpredictable because rooms, enemies, items, pickups, and encounters come from a random seed. The overall progression is NOT random: completing objectives, defeating certain bosses, discovering items, and meeting other conditions PERMANENTLY unlocks new content that can appear in future runs.

Principles:
1. Every run is different, but player knowledge and accomplishments carry forward.
2. Unlocks EXPAND THE POSSIBILITY POOL. They do not simply make the player stronger. Unlocking content must never raise the player's baseline stats.
3. Bosses, areas, items, pickups, characters, and mechanics can all be gated behind meaningful achievements.
4. Players gradually uncover the full game through experimentation, exploration, and repeated runs.
5. The game must NOT feel like a linear campaign. Progression changes what CAN happen in future runs, rather than dictating exactly what happens next.
6. PROGRESSION IS HIDDEN FROM THE PLAYER (see "Hidden progression" below).

## Hidden progression (UI rules)
- No list of locked content, no locked-item silhouettes, no progress bars or counters toward unlocks, no hints about conditions, and no "unlock conditions" text anywhere in the player-facing UI.
- Content simply starts appearing once unlocked. A subtle, non-specific cue on first discovery is allowed (for example, the item pedestal shows a new item the player has never seen). Never explain why it became available.
- Debug tools (F1 panel) MAY show everything. Debug UI must be clearly separated from player UI and stripped or disabled in release builds.
- A "discovered" log (things the player has already found) is allowed later. It must never reveal undiscovered content.

## Exceptions to the "no linear campaign" principle (kept deliberately small)
1. FIRST RUN (onboarding only): layout, rooms, enemies, and bosses are identical for every player and pinned by data. Items and rewards are still random. It repeats until its goal (defeating the first boss) is reached. Dying, quitting, or abandoning never completes it. Its reward rolls may be restricted to a curated pool and maximum rarity so that no roll makes it unwinnable or trivial.
2. STORY OVERRIDES (rare, used sparingly): a story beat whose flag conditions are met replaces the WHOLE next run with an authored plan. These are for key story moments only. The default for every run is unlock-driven randomness, never a story override.

PART 4: SYSTEMS (DESIGN)
==================================================

## Stats (v1)
StatType: MoveSpeed, JumpHeight, CharacterSize, FireRate, Damage, ProjectileSpeed, Range, Luck.
Value = (base + sum of flats) x (1 + sum of percent adds) x product of multipliers. Modifiers carry a Source so they can be removed. Adding a stat = appending one enum value.
CharacterSize changes collider, eye height, hitbox, and projectile spawn point (clamped 0.5 to 2.0). The transform is never scaled.

## Health (Isaac-style hearts, half-heart units)
- Red containers: main health. Soul hearts (Fellowship): temporary overlay. Dark hearts (Corruption): overlay that bursts on break.
- Overlay absorbs damage first, then red. Short invincibility frames after taking a hit.
- Health and hearts were implemented by the Assistant. READ the existing code before extending it, and do not create a second health system.

## Items
An item = stat modifiers + shot recipe edits + tags (+ later, event hooks).
- ItemDefinition (ScriptableObject): id, displayName, description, tags, statModifiers, recipe edits (projectile count, spread, pierce, shot size multiplier, homing, burn dps and duration, damage type override), plus rarity, pools, weightMultiplier, and requiredUnlockId.
- requiredUnlockId: empty means always available. A non-empty value means the item only appears once that unlock is granted.
- ShotRecipe is built from stats every throw, edited by held items and active synergies, then clamped by ClampToCaps. Caps exist on projectile count, pierce, homing, size, speed, range, and burn.
- Synergies are data: SynergyDefinition (tag, requiredCount, bonus). N or more held items with a tag grants the bonus automatically.
- ItemLoadout is the plain C# logic behind PlayerInventory (unit tested). Items apply in pickup order.

## Item rarity, pools, and luck
- ItemRarity: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Legendary. Base weights 60, 28, 10, 2.
- Luck adjustment: clamp Luck to -5..10. For rarity tier t (Common=0 .. Legendary=3), effective weight = baseWeight x max(0.2, 1 + 0.12 x Luck x t).
- ItemPoolType: Normal, Treasure, Boss, Shop, Dark, Holy. Dark and Holy are reserved for the Corruption and Fellowship path and may be empty.
- ItemPoolRoller rolls a rarity first (only among rarities with eligible items), then an item within that rarity by weightMultiplier. It filters by pool, exclusions (owned or already shown), and locked items. If the pool is empty it falls back to Normal; if that is also empty it returns null. It never throws and is fully deterministic for the same inputs.
- Unlock gating: items whose requiredUnlockId is not granted never roll.

## Item pedestals
- ItemPedestal (lives in WD.Player): rolls an item from a pool using RunSession.Rng, shows a floating rarity-tinted orb and a label with name and description when the player is near and looking at it. Press E to take it (adds to PlayerInventory, calls RunSession.PickItem).
- Pedestals in a group (same groupId) offer different items, and taking one empties the others in the group (Isaac-style treasure room choice).
- Does not work while the cursor is unlocked (debug panel open). No pricing or shops yet.

## Randomness (implemented)
- RunRandom: per-stream independent generators. Drawing from one stream never changes another. Same seed gives the same sequence.
- RandomStream (current): Items, Rewards, Encounter, Generation. Only APPEND new streams at the end. Combat and Events streams are expected later.
- RunSession (static): Seed, Rng, PickedItemIds, events RunStarted and ItemPicked. StartRun(seed = 0): 0 means a fresh random seed, any other number replays that seed exactly.
- REQUIRED behavior (being applied): RunSession must NOT use a fixed default seed. It starts fresh, logs "[Run] Seed <n>", and resets ALL static state (including event subscriptions) on entering Play mode.
- Story-critical content is pinned by DATA, never by a magic run-seed number, because results for a given run seed change whenever item data or pools change.

## Level design (authored seeds with random rolls; Elden Ring Nightreign style)
- Floors are authored Floor Seeds (hand-built layouts, several per floor) with random rolls inside slots: encounters, rewards, boss, hazards, events, paths, atmosphere.
- Each slot has a weighted roll table and a difficulty budget. Luck and Alignment can shift the weights.
- Tags can require or exclude options (for example, no fire enemies in a flooded room).
- Terms: RUN SEED = the number that drives random rolls. FLOOR SEED = an authored layout. Do not confuse them.
- Floor seed selection is unlock-driven by default: a weighted random pick among the floor seeds that are unlocked.

## Run start (resolution order)
1. Story override: the highest-priority story beat whose required flags are all set and forbidden flags are all clear. It replaces the WHOLE next run (ties broken by id, alphabetical).
2. First run: if "first_run_complete" is not set, use the authored first-run plan (identical layout, rooms, enemies, bosses; random items; consumed only when the first boss is defeated).
3. Otherwise: for each floor, a weighted random pick among unlocked floor seeds (RandomStream.Generation).
A story beat is consumed when its milestone is reached (default: floor boss reached). Until then it repeats.

## Unlock system (the heart of meta-progression)
- UNLOCKS are permanent, stored in save data, and unlock content for future runs. Unlock IDs are strings.
- Gateable content: items, pickups, characters, floor seeds, rooms and room types, bosses, enemies, events, mechanics, floors, and endings. Each carries an optional unlock ID; pools skip anything locked.
- Unlock conditions are DATA (UnlockDefinition ScriptableObjects) built from gameplay events: boss defeated, item picked, run won, room cleared, floor reached, alignment reached, achievements in a single run, and combinations of these.
- Unlocks expand the pool. They never raise baseline stats.
- Keep STORY FLAGS and UNLOCK IDS separate in concept. Story flags drive story beats and first-run status. Unlock IDs drive content availability. Both persist between runs. Endings can need both.
- Persistence: a small JSON save in Application.persistentDataPath. A missing or corrupt file must start empty and NEVER crash the game. Write atomically.

## Alignment (later)
Corruption vs Fellowship, tracked as values shifted by choices (item picks, sacrifices, boss and NPC decisions). Thresholds gate item pools, visuals, NPCs, and endings. Built on the same stat, tag, and unlock systems.

## Game feel
GameFeel (static helper) provides hit-stop, screen shake, particle bursts, and generated placeholder sounds. Its settings (ShakeScale, HitStopScale, SoundVolume) will be exposed in a settings menu later, including an option to turn shake and hit-stop off. Head bob is smoothed and toggleable.
